# Economy and Food Allocation

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§10, 24, 25, 39 and §41J, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

This note owns requisitions, rationing, exchange and allocation policy. [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Food Production|Food Production]] owns physical food production. The claim that subsequent food collapse kills more people than the exchange is a provisional scenario assumption, not an established mortality calculation.

## Food Crisis

Food becomes the main driver of political transformation.

The immediate war kills large numbers of people.

The working scenario anticipates greater mortality from the subsequent food collapse. The comparison remains provisional until losses are bounded.

The September timing creates a mixed situation:

- some crops have already matured
- harvesting is disrupted
- transport collapses
- fuel becomes scarce
- storage and refrigeration fail
- food-processing industries stop
- warehouses become strategic assets

The state rapidly requisitions:

- grain
- seed
- fertilizer
- livestock
- preserved food
- feedstocks
- farm machinery
- diesel
- distribution centres

Food rationing begins rapidly.

Private hoarding becomes a serious offence.

## Postwar Economy

The economy likely evolves through overlapping phases rather than clean ideological systems.

### Early phase

Dominated by:

- requisition
- rationing
- emergency labour
- central allocation
- salvage
- barter
- black markets

### Stabilization phase

Mixed system including:

- state-directed strategic industry
- rationing
- controlled private exchange
- local markets
- informal barter
- labour assignment
- ration credits

### Reconstruction phase

**Status: Provisional**

**Development: Post-2000 outcomes explicitly deferred**

Likely revival of:

- currency
- regulated private enterprise
- cooperatives
- municipal industry
- state corporations
- trade

The eventual system does not need to recreate prewar capitalism exactly.

Hydro and other strategic industries may remain much more centralised.

## Currency

**Status: Exploring**

OPEN QUESTION.

Possibilities include:

- continued use of Canadian dollars
- emergency provincial notes
- ration certificates
- labour credits
- dual currency
- later independent Québec currency

Currency may become secondary during peak famine.

Food, fuel, medicine, and work entitlements may matter more than cash.

## Likely Strategic Doctrine

**Status: Provisional**

A useful implied hierarchy of postwar state priorities:

1. food
2. clean water
3. electrical power
4. sanitation
5. communications
6. rail and river transport
7. medicine
8. strategic manufacturing
9. housing
10. civilian consumption

Possible reconstruction doctrine:

> **Hydro → rail → telecommunications → agriculture → industry → consumer society**

This is conceptual, not yet canonical wording.
