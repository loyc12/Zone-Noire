# Government and Emergency Rule

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§5, 6, 26, 27 and §41B, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

This note owns constitutional continuity, political succession and relations among the three major power centres. Lévesque's initial leadership, death several years after the war and its symbolic role in the loss of prewar democratic continuity are adopted directions. Around 1987 remains a provisional date. Individual laws and succession mechanisms remain open.

## Government Continuity

René Lévesque remains premier at the time of the war.

The provincial government initially attempts to preserve constitutional continuity.

Early assumptions may include:

- Canada still formally exists
- federal authority may eventually recover
- emergency measures are temporary
- Québec remains a province in legal theory
- federal assets are being "temporarily administered" rather than permanently seized

In practice, federal authority rapidly becomes irrelevant.

The Québec government progressively assumes control over:

- federal depots
- communications facilities
- military stores
- transport assets
- food stocks
- fuel stocks
- police coordination
- surviving federal personnel
- border control
- economic allocation

The transition to de facto sovereignty should be gradual and administrative rather than immediately ideological.

This creates room for legal ambiguity for several years.

## René Lévesque and Political Legitimacy

Lévesque serves as the main surviving link to the prewar democratic order.

His importance is partly constitutional and partly symbolic.

During the first years:

- emergency decrees are still issued by an elected government
- the population remembers normal electoral politics
- old ministries and parliamentary institutions formally continue
- authoritarian measures remain framed as temporary emergency necessities

Lévesque dies several years after the war, likely around **1987**, close to the timing of his real-world death.

His death functions as a major symbolic turning point.

After his death:

- elections are postponed indefinitely
- succession occurs through emergency institutional mechanisms
- government legitimacy shifts from democratic continuity to survival performance
- coercive institutions become less constrained by prewar political norms

Possible historiographical distinction:

- **La Continuité** — pre-Lévesque-death emergency period
- **Le Gouvernement d'urgence** — later authoritarian period

Both terms are PROVISIONAL.

## Authoritarian Government

The authoritarian regime should emerge incrementally.

Drivers include:

- famine
- refugee pressure
- public-health emergencies
- infrastructure collapse
- military necessity
- food requisition
- labour shortages
- transportation scarcity

Measures may include:

- rationing
- compulsory labour
- travel permits
- curfews
- border control
- military courts
- press control
- property seizure
- settlement consolidation
- industrial assignment
- conscription

Each policy may initially have a plausible emergency justification.

Collectively, they produce an authoritarian command society.

The regime's moral ambiguity is important.

It should be possible to argue both:

- many policies prevented greater mortality
- serious abuses and crimes were committed under their cover

## Political Power Centres

By the late emergency period, three major institutional blocs likely dominate.

### Civil administration

Derived from:

- prewar ministries
- civil service
- surviving politicians
- regional administrators
- municipal governments

Main interests:

- legal continuity
- civilian legitimacy
- tax and ration administration
- social order

### Military/security establishment

Derived from:

- armed forces
- police
- emergency militias
- border forces

Main interests:

- territorial security
- logistics
- public order
- defence of strategic assets

### Hydro / technical establishment

Derived from:

- Hydro-Québec
- public works
- engineering
- telecommunications
- transport technicians

Main interests:

- infrastructure
- energy allocation
- industrial reconstruction
- technical continuity

Their relationships should be negotiated rather than reduced to simple factional warfare.
