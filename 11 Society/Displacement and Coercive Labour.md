# Displacement and Coercive Labour

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§14, 15, 16, 17, 18 and §41E, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

Internal displacement, external refugee arrivals, border militarisation and the emergence of forced labour are adopted early-setting directions. Specific admission rules, institutional forms and names remain proposals. Scarcity helps explain policy choices without proving that exclusion or coercion is necessary.

## Refugee Crisis

The refugee crisis begins with internal displacement.

Early refugees include:

- Montréal residents
- southern Québec residents
- communities near contaminated targets
- people fleeing infrastructure collapse

The government initially responds using:

- schools
- arenas
- churches
- barracks
- municipal shelters
- temporary camps

The situation becomes qualitatively worse when refugees begin entering from:

- Ontario
- New England
- other northeastern American regions

The surviving Québec food system cannot support unrestricted immigration.

The southern frontier therefore becomes increasingly militarised.

Progression:

1. emergency checkpoints
2. controlled crossings
3. refugee screening
4. border closure
5. fortified crossing points
6. interdiction of unauthorized entry

This is one of the main drivers of postwar authoritarianism.

## Refugee Triage

**Status: Provisional**

Admission is progressively based on perceived social utility and humanitarian priority.

Possible priority categories:

- children
- close family of residents
- doctors
- nurses
- engineers
- machinists
- farmers
- electricians
- technicians
- pregnant women
- specialised industrial workers

This creates an explicit or implicit hierarchy of human value.

It also produces:

- corruption
- forged credentials
- patronage
- black-market border crossings
- smuggling
- family separation
- resentment toward perceived privileged classes

## Forced Labour and Camp System

Refugee camps gradually transform into coercive labour institutions.

Original function:

- shelter
- registration
- food distribution
- health screening
- temporary resettlement

Later functions:

- labour allocation
- agricultural work
- forestry
- construction
- salvage
- railway repair
- industrial labour

Residents may be promised:

- permanent residency
- citizenship
- resettlement
- eventual repatriation
- family reunification
- labour credits

Possible concept:

- **Crédits d'établissement** ( Crédits ) — PROVISIONAL
- **Centre de Restoration Agricole** ( CDRA ) — PROVISIONAL

The system gradually becomes forced labour in practice.

Conditions are often miserable.

Mortality can result from:

- malnutrition
- exposure
- poor sanitation
- disease
- workplace accidents
- violence
- inadequate medical treatment
- punitive ration reductions

The system is not necessarily created as a deliberate extermination mechanism.

Its brutality emerges through the logic of extreme scarcity and bureaucratic coercion.

## Ethnolinguistic Dimension

The camp and refugee system eventually gains a strong anglophone dimension.

This should arise structurally rather than from an immediately explicit anti-anglophone policy.

Reasons include:

- later refugee waves arrive from Ontario and the United States
- francophone Québec residents are more likely to possess local family networks
- citizens receive priority over foreigners
- anglophone newcomers have less political representation
- some southern anglophone Québec communities may also be displaced

Over time, anglophones become disproportionately represented among:

- camp labourers
- agricultural labourers
- resettlement populations
- border detainees
- industrial work units

This can evolve into deliberate discrimination later.

Possible political justifications may involve:

- food scarcity
- security
- citizenship
- cultural preservation
- loyalty concerns

The setting should treat this as a morally serious and historically contested institution rather than merely flavour.

## Industrial Forced Labour

**Status: Exploring**

There may be industrial equivalents to the agricultural camp system.

Possible forms:

- state work camps
- factory dormitory compounds
- assigned industrial labour
- prison-industry systems
- settlement labour brigades

Possible institutional term:

- **Ateliers d'État** — PROVISIONAL
- **Centres d'affectation industrielle** ( CDAI ) — PROVISIONAL

No final terminology has been selected.
