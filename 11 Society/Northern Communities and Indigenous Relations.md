# Northern Communities and Indigenous Relations

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Exploring**

**Source: Revised handoff §§23 and §41I, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

Northern communities and Indigenous political agency are part of the setting context. Detailed political relationships are outside the active 1983–1987 development scope. Their precise postwar agreements and relationships with Québec remain open. This note owns those relationships, while [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Electricity and Communications|Electricity and Communications]] owns the recurring electrical dependencies.

## The North

Northern Québec requires dedicated development.

Important factors:

- James Bay hydroelectric installations
- distance from major strike targets
- difficulty of resupply
- infrastructure dependence
- Cree and Inuit political autonomy
- local knowledge
- northern transport

The catastrophe could increase the bargaining power of northern Indigenous governments.

Québec's access to northern generation during the early crisis is unresolved. The author suspects that distance and infrastructure damage may make northern installations effectively inaccessible for much of the first decade. The actual 1983 generating and transmission network, damage, repair capacity and local political access need research before this becomes a setting fact.

However, the central government lacks the manpower to directly dominate enormous northern territories.

Later contact could produce a postwar political settlement involving:

- Québec
- Cree governments
- Inuit governments
- northern Hydro administrations

Possible concept:

- a Québec–Cree–Inuit compact

No final structure has been decided.

Early communication and practical cooperation should not be assumed merely from prewar jurisdiction or ownership of electrical assets. The northern political settlement remains deferred beyond the current period.

This area should not be treated as empty northern hinterland.
