# Everyday Life and Cultural Change

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Exploring**

**Source: Revised handoff §§36, 37 and §41MO, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

The adopted material context includes uneven services, poverty and surviving technical knowledge. Language, religion and ordinary-life detail remain avenues for development. The setting does not determine the narrative's language or voice.

## Language and Culture

OPEN DEVELOPMENT AREA.

Likely effects:

- greater isolation from France
- reduced exposure to global English-language media
- large anglophone refugee populations
- military and industrial vocabulary entering common speech
- new technical and administrative jargon
- stronger regional dialect divergence

Possible long-term result:

Québec French remains recognisably Québécois but develops a substantial body of postwar vocabulary.

## Religion

OPEN DEVELOPMENT AREA.

The Quiet Revolution remains part of prewar history.

The catastrophe does not necessarily restore Catholic institutional dominance.

However, Québec City's survival may generate:

- folk religious interpretations
- new cults of providence
- pilgrimage culture
- renewed church charity networks
- secular civic mythologization

Religion should develop heterogeneously.

## Early Generations

**Status: Provisional**

Possible broad cohorts:

### Prewar generation

Remember normal 1970s–1980s life.

Often more politically conservative about emergency institutions because they directly experienced collapse.

### War children

Born shortly before or after 1983.

Grew up under famine, authoritarianism, rationing, and reconstruction.

Likely central to democratisation.

Later generational politics and retrospective memory belong in [[99 Workshop/Later Scenarios|Later Scenarios]].
