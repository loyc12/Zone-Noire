# Transport and Industrial Capacity

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§21, 22, 28 and §41N, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

Petroleum scarcity, uneven industrial regression and surviving technical knowledge shape the early setting. Rail and electrification become strategic priorities. Recovery depends on food surplus, skilled labour, inputs and maintainable equipment, without a selected completion date.

## Transportation

Transport becomes one of the main reconstruction bottlenecks.

Québec has abundant hydroelectric potential but lacks domestic petroleum production sufficient to preserve prewar mobility.

Petroleum constraints affect:

- tractors
- trucks
- aviation
- military vehicles
- construction equipment
- chemical production
- lubricants
- plastics
- mining
- transport

This strongly incentivizes electrification.

Likely priorities:

1. hydroelectric generation
2. transmission
3. rail
4. telecommunications
5. agriculture
6. heavy industry
7. civilian consumption

Railways become strategically central.

Likely developments:

- restoration of freight rail
- railway electrification
- expansion of passenger rail
- limited road maintenance
- rationed motor vehicles
- use of rivers and the St. Lawrence for transport
- revival of animal traction in rural areas

## Technology Regression

The setting should avoid a clean return to the 19th century.

The more realistic model is **salvage industrialism** or **deindustrialised technical society**.

People retain:

- scientific knowledge
- books
- machine tools
- electrical motors
- radios
- vehicles
- industrial materials
- modern engineering concepts

They lose:

- global supply chains
- cheap replacement parts
- petrochemicals
- specialised electronics
- industrial-scale pharmaceuticals
- precision manufacturing
- abundant transport

A rural settlement in the 1990s might therefore contain:

- horse-drawn carts
- repaired 1970s tractors
- communal electrical lighting
- radio communications
- welded salvage equipment
- scarce chainsaws
- mechanical workshops
- almost no private fuel use

Technological development becomes highly uneven.

## Recovery

Recovery should eventually become substantial.

If Québec preserves:

- literacy
- organised government
- agricultural knowledge
- universities or technical schools
- hydroelectric power
- machine tools
- engineers
- basic industrial capacity

then full civilizational collapse should not remain permanent.

Important principle:

> Technical knowledge survives more easily than industrial capacity.

Once food production stabilises, specialisation can rapidly return.

Recovery loop:

food surplus
→ specialised labour
→ better maintenance
→ greater production
→ expanded industrial capacity
→ further surplus.

The most severe bottleneck is likely the first decade.
