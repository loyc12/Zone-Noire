# Health, Water and Sanitation

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§9 and §41G, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

The collapse of medical supply, water and sanitation systems is adopted setting context. The following shortages and disease risks are scenario working material, not researched local incidence, a mortality estimate or medical guidance.

## Collapse of Healthcare and Sanitation

The healthcare crisis is not simply a shortage of doctors.

The support system of modern medicine disappears extremely quickly.

Critical shortages include:

- pharmaceuticals
- antibiotics
- insulin
- anaesthetics
- sterile supplies
- laboratory reagents
- blood products
- vaccines
- oxygen
- refrigeration
- disposable equipment
- replacement medical machinery

Hospitals simultaneously face:

- burns
- trauma
- radiation exposure
- displaced populations
- infectious disease
- sanitation failure
- malnutrition

Water and sewage systems are major vulnerabilities.

Likely crises include:

- contaminated drinking water
- sewage backup
- overloaded latrines
- dysentery
- respiratory epidemics
- lice
- tuberculosis
- wound infections
- dramatically increased maternal mortality
- dramatically increased infant mortality

Public-health officials, water engineers, chemists, and sanitation specialists become strategically important.

The author expects infant mortality to be a substantial part of the 1984–1985 crisis. Its magnitude and causes have not been modelled. Distinguish deaths from disease, malnutrition, failed medical care and exposure before deriving a demographic estimate.
