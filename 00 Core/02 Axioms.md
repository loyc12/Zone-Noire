# Axioms

**Type: Design constraints**

**Status: Selected baseline**

**Scope: Realism constraint and stable design principles adopted from the context handoff**

**Approval: Batch-two setting alignment, recorded in [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|Integration Decisions]]**

## Realism in Consequences

Physical, ecological, technological and social consequences must support the setting's realistic premise. The selected disaster scenario is fiction. Research evidence, scenario assumptions, inferred consequences and selected fictional facts remain distinguishable. Importing the handoff does not independently verify its historical or technical claims.

Realism here means avoiding clear conflicts with known history and modelable constraints as best we can, rather than reconstructing every detail with academic precision. Plausible approximations and authorial judgements may settle questions where direct evidence is sparse, provided their uncertainty remains visible.

## Uneven Destruction and Survival

Destruction and radioactive contamination vary by location. Some places are annihilated, others contaminated, and others physically intact but deprived of supplies, markets and services. Surviving buildings or generating stations do not instantaneously restablish a functioning social or industrial system.

Radiation, climate disruption, famine and infrastructure failure are distinct systems with interacting consequences. Neither southern Québec nor the entire continent becomes uniformly uninhabitable.

## Infrastructure Has Material Dependencies

Grid collapse has multiple causes, including physical destruction, instability, lost communications and personnel, and unavailable replacement equipment. EMP may contribute but cannot serve as the universal explanation.

Restored capability requires equipment, maintenance, skilled people, transport and inputs. Hydroelectric potential alone does not supply a complete grid, functioning railways, modern medicine or political access to northern territory.

## Climate Shock Is Severe and Temporary

The scenario includes a severe multiyear disruption of growing conditions. Québec does not remain permanently frozen. The timing and magnitude require a bounded model. Climatic recovery, agricultural recovery and the end of food scarcity need not occur together.

## Knowledge Outlasts Capacity

Loss of supply chains and specialised production occurs faster than loss of scientific and technical knowledge. Survivors retain books, skills and uneven stocks of modern equipment. Material regression therefore combines salvage, improvised repair and older techniques rather than a uniform return to a historical technological era.

Recovery can build on food surplus, specialisation and surviving institutions. It must reflect actual bottlenecks and does not have a guaranteed completion date.

## Institutions and Political Choices Matter

Real Québec institutions and local conditions shape the setting. Hydro-Québec, civilian administration, security organisations, municipalities and Indigenous governments have different capacities and interests. Northern communities have agency and cannot be treated as empty hinterland.

The adopted authoritarian trajectory develops through decisions, emergency powers and institutional incentives. Scarcity does not establish that every coercive measure is necessary, effective or inevitable. Forced labour develops through policy escalation, with abuses and affected populations treated seriously.

Institutional survival and grave wrongdoing can coexist. The regime's claims about lives saved must remain distinguishable from demonstrated outcomes. Neither permanent dictatorship nor eventual democracy is required by the design constraints.

## Future Outcomes Remain Contingent

The early setting does not have to produce the brainstorm's particular 2023 society. Post-2000 projections remain explicitly deferred and may change substantially as earlier events develop.
