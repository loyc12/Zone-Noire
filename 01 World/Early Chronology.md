# Early Chronology

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§2, 29, 40, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

The broad sequence is immediate survival, famine and coercive emergency government, then uneven stabilisation and reconstruction. September 1983 is selected. The precise exchange date and all subsequent dates below are provisional placements imported from the handoff, not separately approved events or deadlines.

**Scope: 1983–1999, with the source's overlapping 1995–2000 range retained**

**Dating: Provisional except the selected month and year of the exchange**

This note owns timing and sequence. Detailed causes and institutions belong in the linked topical owners. Inclusion here does not approve a name, mechanism or numerical claim that remains open there. Post-2000 events belong in [[99 Workshop/Later Scenarios|Later Scenarios]].

## 26 September 1983 — Nuclear Exchange

- Soviet false alarm escalates
- NATO–Warsaw Pact nuclear exchange begins
- Montréal and Ottawa are struck
- Major North American infrastructure collapses
- Québec City survives a failed intended strike
- Provincial emergency measures begin

## 27–30 September 1983

- communications collapse
- hospitals overwhelmed
- food and fuel requisitioned
- Hydro begins grid isolation
- military coordination begins
- initial refugee movement starts

## October 1983

- southern Québec evacuations intensify
- rationing formalized
- federal assets placed under provincial control
- refugee camps created
- military-police coordination expands
- border checkpoints appear

## November–December 1983

- sanitation crisis worsens
- non-essential motor traffic restricted
- food distribution centralised
- grid repair prioritises strategic zones
- livestock slaughter expands
- labour obligations introduced

## Winter 1983–1984

- mass mortality from cold, famine, infection, and lack of medicine
- remote regions become isolated
- industrial salvage begins
- practical Canadian federal authority disappears

## Spring 1984

- centrally directed planting campaign
- seed and fuel allocated
- refugee labour mobilized
- agricultural contamination mapping begins

## Summer 1984

- severe climatic disruption
- agricultural collapse
- ration levels reduced
- compulsory labour expands
- internal movement increasingly controlled

## Winter 1984–1985

- demographic low point
- black markets expand
- food theft becomes endemic
- military tribunals grow
- some isolated communities abandoned

## 1985–1986

- administrative reconstruction districts formalized
- Hydro's mandate expands
- rail restoration prioritised
- agricultural output stabilises at low levels

## ~1987

- René Lévesque dies
- national mourning
- elections postponed indefinitely
- emergency succession takes place
- authoritarian emergency state consolidates

## 1988–1990

- civilian, military, and Hydro elites dominate government
- CDRA and CDAI system expands
- post-Canadian political identity becomes unavoidable
- separate monetary arrangements may emerge

## 1990–1995

- climate improves
- famine gradually gives way to chronic scarcity
- electrical islands reconnect
- local manufacturing increases
- border control becomes institutionalized
- camp system reaches maturity

## 1995–2000

- reconstruction accelerates
- technical schools expand
- rail and industrial production improve
- urban populations stabilise
- external trade gradually resumes
- political justification for permanent emergency rule weakens
