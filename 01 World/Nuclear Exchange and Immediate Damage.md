# Nuclear Exchange and Immediate Damage

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§3 and §41A, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

The September 1983 exchange and major continental disruption are selected in [[00 Core/01 Premise|Premise]]. This note owns the conflict assumptions and damage model. The precise escalation chain is deliberately unspecified; the target list, weapons and local effects remain open.

## Point of Departure

The real Soviet nuclear false alarm is the divergence point. In this timeline, erroneous detections, mishandled warnings and poor decisions lead to a Soviet first strike followed by a full two-way US–Soviet nuclear exchange. Launches occur shortly after the initial warnings, without an approved minute-by-minute sequence or precise exchange date. Events outside northeastern North America need only enough definition to constrain local consequences. “The event” is the author's working shorthand and not approved in-universe terminology.

**Status: Selected baseline**

**Scope: Fictional scenario premise; historical and technical plausibility unverified**

Corruption-related undermaintenance of Soviet nuclear ICBMs causes many failures in this timeline. The prevalence, affected missile systems and failure modes require research. This premise does not establish a numerical failure rate or particular target outcome.

## The Nuclear Exchange

### Immediate strategic consequences

The nuclear war should remain broadly realistic rather than over-specified unless a future target-map document establishes exact strikes.

Montréal, Vancouver, Halifax, Ottawa–Gatineau and Toronto are the author's intended group of severely affected Canadian cities. Their precise strikes and losses remain to be assessed. Other regionally important population, industrial and trade centres are also targeted unevenly. Likely target categories include:

- Montréal
- Ottawa
- Toronto
- major northeastern American cities
- Canadian military bases
- airbases
- command-and-control facilities
- industrial hubs
- transportation chokepoints
- selected electrical infrastructure
- possibly Québec City and/or the Valcartier military complex

The exact strike list remains **OPEN**.

Important design principle:

> Nuclear destruction should be highly uneven.

Some areas are annihilated, some are contaminated, some suffer only indirect collapse.

Some retain considerable physical infrastructure but lose access to fuel, medicine, transportation, spare parts, and markets.
