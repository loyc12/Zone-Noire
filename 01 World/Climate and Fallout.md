# Climate and Fallout

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current direction**

**Source: Revised handoff §§11, 13, imported under [[00 Core/03 Integration Decisions|the batch-two decision]]**

Severe but temporary climatic disruption and uneven fallout are the selected scenario direction. Temperature, deposition and duration values are not established by this import. The technical statements below are inherited working context, not a completed effects study.

## Nuclear Winter / Climatic Disruption

The postwar climate enters a severe multiyear disruption.

Exact global temperature figures should remain undefined unless future research establishes a specific war model.

Relevant consequences:

- colder summers
- abnormal frost
- reduced sunlight
- shorter growing seasons
- lower precipitation in some areas
- unpredictable rainfall
- reduced crop maturation
- several consecutive poor harvests

The first postwar summer, especially **1984**, may be remembered as the worst agricultural year.

Possible term:

- **"l'Été Noir"** — PROVISIONAL

Possible broader term:

- **"le Grand Hiver"** — PROVISIONAL

The climatic effects should diminish progressively over several years rather than remain permanently catastrophic.

## Radioactive Contamination

Terminology note:

Food is primarily **radioactively contaminated**, not merely "irradiated".

Important radionuclide concerns include:

- iodine-131 in the immediate aftermath
- caesium-137 for long-term environmental contamination
- strontium-90 for long-term food-chain and skeletal exposure

Strontium-90 is especially politically important because it behaves chemically like calcium and can enter milk, bones, and teeth.

Contamination should be highly uneven.

Southern Québec may contain:

- severe fallout corridors
- relatively clean districts
- contaminated agricultural zones
- abandoned regions

This can create formal agricultural classifications.

Possible provisional classification:

- **Zone blanche** — monitored/acceptable
- **Zone grise** — restricted agriculture
- **Zone noire** — prohibited or heavily restricted settlement
- **Zone rouge/orange/jaune/verte** — Could later be used for further categorization

These names are PROVISIONAL.

Future documentation should establish:

- strike map
- prevailing winds
- fallout corridors
- long-term exclusion areas
- resettlement policy

## Unresolved Timing

The source describes effects diminishing over several years, improvement during 1990–1995, and climatic normalisation in its late-1990s–2000s arc. These timings have not been reconciled. Climate recovery, crop recovery and the end of famine need separate dates. Do not extend the climate shock merely to fit an institutional timeline.
