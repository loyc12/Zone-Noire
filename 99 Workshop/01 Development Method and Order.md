# Development Method and Order

**Type: Authorial guide**

**Status: Active**

This guide supports setting development within [[00 Core/01 Premise|Premise]]. Current work focuses on the September 1983 event through [[12 People/René Lévesque|Lévesque]]'s death, provisionally around 1987. It establishes no additional fictional facts and does not lift the deferral of development after 1990. Use [[99 Workshop/Research/Research Topics|Research Topics]] to choose a question and [[99 Workshop/Research/Research Method|Research Method]] to investigate and record it.

## Authorial Selection

Research bounds plausible outcomes without requiring exact historical data for every detail. When sources are sparse, propose a supported range or rounded figure and state its uncertainty. Consequential fictional choices go to the author, including a choice to settle an uncertain value for thematic reasons. Record authorised results in their topical owners and update affected dependencies, while keeping supporting evidence and gaps in the topic's research note.

## Suggested Dependency Order

Begin with enough temporal and geographic scope to make research relevant. Make conflict assumptions explicit before deriving local consequences. Use those consequences to examine infrastructure and community conditions. Develop narrative plans alongside that work as the author's focus becomes clearer.

This is a proposed working order, not a selected disaster history or a requirement to complete the world before writing. Develop enough detail to resolve the current question. Propose dates, targets, casualty ranges, institutions or technical outcomes when useful, and label unsupported precision and unresolved assumptions. Do not present a proposal as established history or selected setting fact.

Keep research comparisons and calculations in Workshop or beside their topic. Selected setting answers belong in their topical owners. Author-facing narrative planning belongs in `80 Narrative` when needed. Corpus prose remains entirely user-written.
