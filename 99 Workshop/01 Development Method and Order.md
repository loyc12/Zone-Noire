# Development Method and Order

**Type: Authorial guide**

**Status: Active**

This method supports author-facing development when the author resumes it. It establishes no fictional canon and does not lift the deferral recorded in [[00 Core/01 Premise|Premise]].

## Working Loop

1. State the question, its owner, and the scope authorised for the current task.
2. Identify existing constraints, open dependencies, and alternatives. Separate real-world evidence from scenario assumptions.
3. Research the question at the geographic and temporal scale it requires. Record sources and limitations. Leave unsupported outcomes open.
4. Trace relevant consequences under explicit assumptions. Distinguish estimates and inferences from observed facts and author-selected fiction.
5. Present consequential choices for authorial selection. Record authorised results in their topical owners with their actual status and update dependent notes.

## Knowledge Layers

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Objective reality | What occurs under the selected conditions? |
| Institutional knowledge | What can relevant organisations establish or reliably do? |
| Common practice | What can people successfully do in ordinary life? |
| Common belief | What do people believe, including disagreement or error? |
| Narrative revelation | What does the reader learn, and when? |

## Suggested Dependency Order

Begin with enough temporal and geographic scope to make research relevant. Make conflict assumptions explicit before deriving local consequences. Use those consequences to examine infrastructure and community conditions. Develop narrative plans alongside that work as the author's focus becomes clearer.

This is a proposed working order, not a selected disaster history or a requirement to complete the world before writing. Develop enough detail to resolve the current question. Do not invent dates, targets, casualty figures, institutions, or technical outcomes to fill gaps.

Keep research comparisons and calculations in Workshop or beside their topic. Selected setting answers belong in their topical owners. Author-facing narrative planning belongs in `80 Narrative` when needed. Corpus prose remains entirely user-written.
