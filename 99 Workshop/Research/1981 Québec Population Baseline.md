# 1981 Québec Population Baseline

**Type: Research note**

**Status: Exploring**

**Scope: Pre-event population, places and age structure**

## Question and inputs

What population was present before the event, and which 1981 records can anchor later exodus and food-demand ranges? The 1981 census precedes the divergence by roughly two years. A selected pre-event baseline belongs in [[01 World/Early Chronology|Early Chronology]]; later health and mortality estimates belong in [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Health, Water and Sanitation|Health, Water and Sanitation]].

## Evidence

- [Statistics Canada, farm and non-farm populations, 1921–2001](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/95f0303x/t/html/4153161-eng.htm), retrospective census series: Québec's 1981 enumerated population was **6,438,403 people**. Its farm-population estimate is **205,300**, while [another Statistics Canada historical table](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/95-633-x/2007000/t/6500026-eng.htm) gives **203,795**. Both support roughly **200,000 farm residents** for broad planning; resolve the difference only if it materially affects a later estimate. Neither figure measures people able to produce food after disruption
- [Statistics Canada, 1981 population by age and sex](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/CS93-914-1981.pdf), original census volume 93-914: tables cover five-year age groups for census divisions, metropolitan areas and agglomerations, with smaller-place coverage at specified ages. This is the main source for later age-sensitive demand and mortality bounds; figures have not yet been extracted
- [Statistics Canada, Québec census divisions and subdivisions](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.837068/publication.html), original 1981 profile series, Québec volume CS98-905: source for municipality and regional baseline counts. The catalogue has a 14.42 MB scan; detailed geography remains to be extracted
- [Statistics Canada, 1981 census tract profiles](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.838185/publication.html), original local-area profiles: useful for separating Montréal's core, fringe and nearby receiving areas once a bounded exodus question is chosen

## Analysis and uncertainty

Québec-wide totals constrain scale, but cannot stand in for September 1983 population or the number of survivors. Census metropolitan areas, municipalities, census divisions and later regional units have different boundaries. A later comparison must carry the 1981 geography and age definitions through explicitly. Real-world 1986 counts occur after the timeline's divergence and cannot be used as post-event observations.

## Choices and outcome

**Gap:** The retrieved census fixes a 1981 total, but this scan has not established a September 1983 count or extracted age groups and local populations. The difference between the two farm-population estimates is too small to affect broad planning

**Recommended provisional gap-filler:** P1 uses **about 6.5 million people in Québec immediately before the event**, reflecting slight growth from the 1981 count of 6.44 million over roughly two years. Keep **about 200,000 farm residents** only as a pre-event census category. Neither figure says how many people survive or can be fed

**Alternatives:** P2 carries **about 6.4 million** directly from the 1981 census when a two-year adjustment adds no useful precision. P3 uses a broad **6.4–6.6 million** pre-event band for sensitivity checks rather than one working figure

**Deferred research:** Extract 1981 place and age tables for specific cities and receiving regions when modelling exodus, food demand or mortality. Check a pre-September 1983 estimate only if the provincial total needs finer precision. Real-world 1986 totals are not post-event observations in this timeline

**Authorial veto / outcome:** Pending. Choose P1, P2, P3 or another justified baseline. No casualty, refugee or carrying-capacity figure is selected here
