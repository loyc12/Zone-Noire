# Research Method

**Type: Authorial guide**

**Status: Active**

Use this method for one bounded question from [[99 Workshop/Research/Research Topics|Research Topics]] at a time. Related small questions may share a pass. [[99 Workshop/00 Current Work Context|Current Work Context]] owns work order, continuation notes, and deferrals. Update the affected thread when pausing or completing a research pass. A research note is evidence and analysis, not setting canon.

1. Define the question, 1983–1987 scope, topical owner and any inputs needed from other topics. If an input is unknown, state a temporary assumption or range
2. Keep the work in one self-contained note in this folder, using [[99 Workshop/Research/Research Note Template|Research Note Template]] if helpful. Its four sections are **Question and inputs**, **Evidence**, **Analysis and uncertainty**, and **Choices and outcome**. Remove prompts that do not apply. Record useful sources, their dates, coverage and limits. Separate historical facts, scenario assumptions, inferences and selected fiction; show units and assumptions for estimates
3. Stop searching when the available evidence is sufficient for a plausible answer or further searches are unlikely to change the decision. Record important dead ends. For a material gap, tell the author what could not be established and offer a few plausible gap-fillers, including a recommended rounded range or best-judgement estimate. State what constrains each useful option and what the choice affects, so the author can exercise an **authorial veto** instead of inheriting an indefinite open question
4. Keep proposed gap-fillers provisional until the author selects one or supplies another. After that selection, update the topical owner and affected dependencies. Label it as an authorial judgement under uncertainty, not a historical measurement, and retain the research note as provenance. Split a topic into more notes only when the material needs it

Spend more effort where a choice controls many later consequences; use coarser estimates for background detail. Each topic owns its supporting evidence. Link only the inputs or conclusions another topic actually needs. If later evidence conflicts with a selected premise, surface the conflict for review rather than silently changing the setting.
