# Research Topics

**Type: Research agenda**

**Status: Active**

**Scope: The September 1983 event through [[12 People/René Lévesque|Lévesque]]'s death, provisionally around 1987**

Choose one numbered topic for a research pass, or batch closely related small topics. Each topic should produce its own note using [[99 Workshop/Research/Research Method|Research Method]] and, when useful, [[99 Workshop/Research/Research Note Template|Research Note Template]]. The inputs below are specific dependencies, not a requirement to finish every earlier topic. Where an input remains unknown, use conditional ranges and state the assumption. A topic can reach a usable, explicitly uncertain recommendation without a complete historical dataset. [[99 Workshop/00 Current Work Context|Current Work Context]] owns work order, continuation notes, pending-choice pointers, and deferrals. This agenda owns the bounded questions and inputs, while research notes own evidence and outcomes.

## Exchange and Immediate Damage

### 1. False Alarm and Missile Reliability

Examine the real 1983 incident only as background for the selected divergence. Assess Soviet ICBM maintenance, corruption claims and plausible failure modes. Identify any evidence that challenges the author's selected undermaintenance premise. The exact escalation chain remains deliberately unspecified. Owner: [[01 World/Nuclear Exchange and Immediate Damage|Nuclear Exchange and Immediate Damage]]

### 2. Targets and Regional Damage Bounds

Test plausible 1983 target categories, strike density and failure-sensitive damage ranges for northeastern North America. Include Montréal, Ottawa–Gatineau, Toronto and Halifax where relevant; cover Vancouver only enough to support the wider Canadian setting. Do not select an exact strike map. Input: topic 1 or explicit alternative failure assumptions. Owner: [[01 World/Nuclear Exchange and Immediate Damage|Nuclear Exchange and Immediate Damage]]

### 3. Québec City, Valcartier and Fires

Assess intended targets, ways the weapons aimed at Québec City could fail to detonate or miss, possible Valcartier survival and whether strikes near the city could produce fires reaching its outskirts. Input: topic 2 damage bounds. Owner: [[01 World/Québec City and Valcartier|Québec City and Valcartier]]

### 4. Montréal and the Urban Exodus

Bound Montréal's initial destruction, surviving districts, secondary hazards and likely pace and routes of departure. Examine what could sustain scavenging, gangs or later suburban return without deciding the city's distant future. Input: topic 2 damage bounds. Owner: [[01 World/Montréal and Southern Québec|Montréal and Southern Québec]]

## Environment, Food and Survival

### 5. Climate Shock

Compare models appropriate to the bounded exchange and identify plausible 1983–1987 temperature, sunlight, growing-season and ecological effects. Separate climatic recovery from food recovery. Input: topic 2 exchange scale or stated ranges. Owner: [[01 World/Climate and Fallout|Climate and Fallout]]

### 6. Fallout Geography

Test deposition and exposure ranges using plausible targets, yields, weather and winds. Examine whether southern districts could be heavily contaminated while much of central Québec remains habitable, without drawing fixed exclusion zones yet. Input: topic 2 or stated strike alternatives. Owner: [[01 World/Climate and Fallout|Climate and Fallout]]

### 7. Food Production and Distribution

Establish the 1983 agricultural baseline, surviving stocks and inputs, planting constraints and possible harvest outcomes. Separate food grown, food transported and food actually accessible to people. Inputs: topics 5–6 where material; independent 1983 baseline research can begin earlier. Owners: [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Food Production|Food Production]] and [[11 Society/Economy and Food Allocation|Economy and Food Allocation]]

### 8. Health and Demographic Bounds

Examine water, sanitation, medical supply, disease, exposure and infant mortality. Test the expected 1984–1985 peak in post-event deaths without assuming a minimum population or fixed death count. Inputs: topics 4 and 7 where material, plus explicit service assumptions. Owners: [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Health, Water and Sanitation|Health, Water and Sanitation]] and [[01 World/Early Chronology|Early Chronology]]

## 1983 Institutions and Capacity

These baseline topics can begin alongside the physical research. Their fictional post-event choices remain open.

### 9. Lévesque and Québec Government

Identify the documented 1983 cabinet, constitutional and emergency powers, referendum politics and Lévesque's political record. Use that evidence to estimate administrative capacity and propose plausible early claims to authority. Present unresolved political choices to the author without selecting his fictional decisions or a successor. Owners: [[11 Society/Government and Emergency Rule|Government and Emergency Rule]] for the institution and [[12 People/René Lévesque|René Lévesque]] for the person summary

### 10. Military and Police

Identify documented 1983 forces, personnel, equipment, command and supply arrangements relevant to Québec. Where detailed records are sparse, use organisational structure and comparable formations to propose bounded estimates. Test how rapidly a mixed emergency force could form, including volunteers and possible later conscription. Input: topic 3 for Valcartier survival when assessing post-event capacity. Owner: [[11 Society/Military and Security|Military and Security]]

### 11. Hydro, Grid and Northern Access

Map the documented 1983 generation and transmission system, then estimate poorly recorded control and repair capacity within explicit assumptions. Distinguish an operating northern plant from power deliverable to southern Québec, and test likely communication and maintenance limits. Local service conclusions need damage assumptions from topics 2–3; the historical network baseline does not. Owners: [[11 Society/Hydro-Québec|Hydro-Québec]] and [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Electricity and Communications|Electricity and Communications]]

## Early Social and Economic Responses

Research the historical and social baseline first. Use conditional physical and institutional assumptions until the earlier topics provide firmer bounds.

### 12. Collective Action, Emergency Rule and Refugees

Examine disaster responses, Québec's contemporary political culture, organised cooperation and resistance, and feasible refugee administration. Test the proposed progression from formal residence or citizenship rules toward language-based exclusion and harsher labour or penal treatment. Examine possible blame directed at Americans and extended to anglophones. Inputs: topics 8–10 where material. Owners: [[11 Society/Government and Emergency Rule|Government and Emergency Rule]] and [[11 Society/Displacement and Coercive Labour|Displacement and Coercive Labour]]

### 13. Rationing, Labour Credits and Currency

Examine how food and other essentials could be allocated, when cash might lose practical value, and what administrative capacity a basic-needs and labour-credit system would require. Leave a later Québec currency as a possible outcome. Inputs: topics 7, 9 and 12. Owner: [[11 Society/Economy and Food Allocation|Economy and Food Allocation]]

### 14. Communications and Regional Contact

Assess surviving radio and transport capacity, then possible early contact with Abitibi, northern Ontario, the Maritimes and northern New England. Identify realistic reasons to exchange information or scarce goods before proposing sustained cooperation. Inputs: topics 2, 10 and 11 where material. Owners: [[11 Society/External Relations and Trade|External Relations and Trade]] and [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Electricity and Communications|Electricity and Communications]]

### 15. Ordinary Life in Selected Communities

Choose a locality or social group before researching housing, heating, schooling, family life, language and everyday services. Use the relevant physical and institutional findings without assuming one experience represents all Québec. Owner: [[11 Society/Everyday Life and Cultural Change|Everyday Life and Cultural Change]]

## Outside the Current Pass

Detailed post-Lévesque institutions, northern political settlements, mature reconstruction and period terminology are retained in their topical owners. The 1988–1990 material is outside current development; development after 1990 remains deferred.
