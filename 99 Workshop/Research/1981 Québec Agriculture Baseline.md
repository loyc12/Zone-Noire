# 1981 Québec Agriculture Baseline

**Type: Research note**

**Status: Exploring**

**Scope: Pre-event farms, crop areas and food-system source map**

## Question and inputs

What agricultural capacity existed before September 1983, and which records can support later food-availability bounds? Selected farm-scale results belong in [[10 Technologies and Infrastructure/Food Production|Food Production]], while food allocation belongs in [[11 Society/Economy and Food Allocation|Economy and Food Allocation]]. This note is a baseline, not a harvest forecast.

## Evidence

- [Statistics Canada, historical Census of Agriculture table](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/95-632-x/2007000/t/4185570-eng.htm), retrospective table with 1981 census data: Québec had **48,144 farms** covering **3,779,169 hectares** of total farm area. The same table separates land in crops and named field crops. Total farm area is not all cropland, and neither measure gives edible food output
- [Statistics Canada, selected grain areas](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/95-632-x/2007000/t/4129751-eng.htm), retrospective 1976–2006 census comparison: leads to individual 1981 grain categories and their areas, useful when testing grain supply rather than treating all fields alike
- [Statistics Canada, corn for silage and tame hay](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/95-632-x/2007000/t/4129752-eng.htm), retrospective census comparison: identifies feed-producing land that cannot simply be counted as direct human food
- [Statistics Canada, original 1981 Census of Agriculture questionnaire](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/sites/default/files/documents/q81-eng.pdf), contemporary instrument: its farm threshold was **$250 or more in agricultural sales in the previous 12 months**. Use its definitions before combining farm, livestock, land-use and production series

## Analysis and uncertainty

The first task is to distinguish land, harvest, livestock feed and food actually delivered to people. A 1981 area figure says little about 1983 stocks, yields, imported inputs, storage, milling, refrigeration, fuel or access after damaged transport. Crop data also require regional mapping because the south is exposed to different damage and fallout assumptions from the centre. Climate and fallout research can be plugged into ranges later without changing this historical source record.

## Choices and outcome

**Gap:** The 1981 census gives farm count and area, but this scan has not established September 1983 stocks, edible output, import dependence or the location of food relative to survivors. Total farm area cannot be converted into calories

**Recommended provisional gap-filler:** A1 uses **roughly 50,000 farms and 3.8 million hectares of farm area** as rounded 1981 proxies for the pre-event 1983 scale. Allow modest two-year drift without modelling it yet. Keep land in crops, feed crops, harvests and delivered food as separate quantities

**Alternatives:** A2 carries the exact 1981 census figures in background calculations, while describing 1983 only as a similar order of magnitude. This avoids implying that 1983 totals were measured

**Deferred research:** Sample regional crop and livestock records, then seek 1982–1983 output, imports, storage and processing evidence. Harvest loss and ration levels depend on climate, fallout and transport assumptions; if stock data stay sparse, offer low, middle and high food-availability ranges for the author's choice

**Authorial veto / outcome:** Pending. Choose A1, A2 or another approach to the pre-event scale. No edible-calorie total, ration level or famine severity is selected here
